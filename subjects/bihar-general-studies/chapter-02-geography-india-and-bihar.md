# Chapter 2 — Geography of India and Bihar

## 1. Read a map before memorizing a place

Use scale, direction, legend, and coordinates. Latitude measures north–south position; longitude measures east–west position. A physical map shows relief and water; a political map shows boundaries; a thematic map may show rainfall, population, crops, or transport.

India extends from the Himalaya in the north to the Indian Ocean in the south. Major physical divisions are the Himalaya, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands. Each affects settlement, river flow, climate, and economic activity.

## 2. Monsoon and climate

India's climate is strongly shaped by seasonal wind reversal. In the southwest monsoon, moist winds bring most annual rainfall to many regions. Relief matters: windward slopes may receive heavy rain while rain-shadow regions receive less. The retreating monsoon affects parts of southeastern India. Rainfall variability affects crops, reservoirs, floods, and drought risk.

Distinguish weather (short-term atmospheric conditions) from climate (long-term pattern). A hot week is weather evidence, not by itself proof of a long-term climate trend.

## 3. Rivers and Bihar

The Ganga flows east across the northern plains. In Bihar it is a major central river and receives tributaries from both sides. Northern tributaries include the Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, and Kosi systems; the Son joins from the south. Himalayan-fed rivers can carry heavy sediment and produce flood risk; embankments, drainage, land use, and settlement patterns affect the impact.

Bihar is landlocked. It borders Nepal to the north, Uttar Pradesh to the west, Jharkhand to the south, and West Bengal to the east. Patna is the state capital. On a blank map, mark the Ganga, major tributaries, neighboring states, and the Nepal border before adding districts.

~~~text
             Nepal
   Himalayan tributaries ↓
UP ---- north Bihar ---- West Bengal
              Ganga  → east
       south Bihar / Son ↑
             Jharkhand
~~~

This is a schematic, not a district map. Use an official map for exact boundaries.

## 4. Agriculture, resources, and settlement

Alluvial plains support intensive agriculture, but crop choice depends on rainfall, irrigation, soil, access to markets, and farm size. Rice, wheat, maize, pulses, and horticulture are important in different parts of Bihar. Do not assume one crop dominates every district. Floodplain fertility can coexist with flood damage and waterlogging.

The Plateau and mineral belts to Bihar's south became part of Jharkhand after state reorganization in 2000. This is a useful historical-geographic distinction when questions compare present Bihar's resources with undivided Bihar.

## 5. Worked map and scale example

A map scale of 1:1,000,000 means 1 cm represents 10 km because 1,000,000 cm equals 10 km. A 7 cm straight-line map distance represents 70 km. Actual road distance may be longer. Always convert units before calculating.

## 6. Questions and explanations

1. Why can a river flood even where its banks are fertile? Sediment enriches soil, while high discharge, siltation, drainage limits, and settlement on the floodplain create risk.
2. Which side of Bihar borders Nepal? The north.
3. Does a physical map primarily show state capitals? No; it shows terrain and natural features.
4. Why is average annual rainfall not enough for crop planning? Timing, intensity, soil, irrigation, and dry spells also matter.
5. What is the difference between weather and climate? Short-term conditions versus long-term statistical patterns.

## 7. Build a physical-to-human geography chain

A geography answer is stronger when it explains a process:

~~~text
relief + climate + water + soil
              -> crops and settlement
              -> transport and livelihoods
              -> exposure to hazards and services
~~~

This chain does not mean nature determines people's lives. Irrigation, markets, infrastructure, institutions, and household resources also change outcomes.

### Relief, drainage, and monsoon

- The Himalaya influence north Indian river systems, rainfall, and the movement of air masses. The Northern Plains are built from river deposits and are densely settled.
- The Peninsular Plateau has older hard-rock terrain and many rivers with different seasonal patterns. The Western and Eastern Ghats affect rainfall distribution.
- A drainage basin is the land area whose water flows to a river and its tributaries. A watershed separates neighboring basins. River order, slope, rainfall, soil, and vegetation affect runoff.
- Monsoon rainfall is seasonal and uneven. **Onset, duration, intensity, dry spells, and withdrawal** matter as much as an annual average.
- Soil terms describe properties, not guaranteed yields. Alluvial soil can support crops but flood, erosion, waterlogging, salinity, or lack of irrigation may limit production.

### Climate and agriculture example

Suppose two blocks receive the same seasonal rainfall total. Block A receives it in moderate rain over many weeks; Block B receives a large share in a few days followed by a long dry spell. The totals match, but crop stress and flood/runoff risk can differ. For an agriculture question, check rainfall timing, irrigation, seed variety, soil, storage, and access to markets.

### Questions with worked reasoning

1. A map shows a tributary joining from the south. What should you check before naming it? **Its location relative to the Ganga and a reliable map legend; the Son is a major southern tributary in Bihar.**
2. Why can an alluvial plain experience both high agricultural value and high flood exposure? **Deposited material can enrich soil, while the same floodplain position exposes settlements and crops to high water.**
3. A rainfall chart gives 1,000 mm for two places. Is their water availability necessarily equal? **No. Timing, runoff, storage, evaporation, soil, and demand differ.**
4. What does a map scale of 1:2,000,000 mean? **1 cm represents 2,000,000 cm = 20 km; 4.5 cm represents 90 km.**
5. A district boundary changed after a map was printed. Which source should settle a current boundary question? **A current official government map or notification.**
6. Why distinguish a watershed from a river channel? **The watershed is the contributing land area; the channel is where water flows.**
7. Name two human factors that affect flood loss. **Housing quality and warning/evacuation access; land use, drainage, services, and vulnerability also matter.**
8. Why should a crop map be read with a rainfall or irrigation map? **Crop distribution reflects water access and seasonality as well as soil and markets.**
