# Chapter 3 — Indian Polity and Governance

## 1. Constitution and core ideas

The Constitution establishes government institutions, allocates powers, protects rights, and sets procedures for public authority. The Preamble describes India as sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic, and republic, and states justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity as goals. These words guide interpretation but do not replace the operative Articles.

Fundamental Rights are enforceable through courts, subject to constitutional limits. Directive Principles guide the state toward social and economic goals but are not directly enforceable in the same way. Fundamental Duties describe civic responsibilities. Questions often test these distinctions.

## 2. Union institutions

Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha. The Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister, is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. The President is the constitutional head of the Union and acts according to constitutional provisions and ministerial advice, with specific discretionary situations defined by convention and law.

The Supreme Court and High Courts interpret law and protect constitutional remedies. The Election Commission conducts elections to Parliament, state legislatures, and the offices specified by the Constitution. The Comptroller and Auditor General audits public accounts. The Public Service Commissions support merit-based recruitment.

## 3. Federalism and Bihar government

India distributes legislative subjects across Union, State, and Concurrent Lists. Parliament and state legislatures make laws within their constitutional fields. The Governor is the constitutional head of a state; the Chief Minister leads the elected government and Council of Ministers responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

Bihar has a Legislative Assembly and a Legislative Council. Local government operates through Panchayati Raj institutions in rural areas and urban local bodies in towns and cities. The 73rd and 74th constitutional amendments strengthened local self-government; reservation, finance, and functions are shaped by constitutional provisions and state law.

## 4. Accountability and public services

Rule of law means public power is exercised under law, not personal preference. Transparency tools, audits, legislative questions, courts, citizen charters, grievance redress, and social audits can expose failures. A policy announcement is not the same as implementation: track eligibility, funding, delivery, access, and outcomes.

## 5. Worked distinction

Question: A policy aims to improve nutrition, but a citizen asks which constitutional category expresses such a state goal. The best distinction is Directive Principles, because they guide policy. If the question asks which rights can be enforced in court, it is asking about Fundamental Rights. Do not infer that a non-justiciable directive is unimportant; it is a constitutional guide to governance.

## 6. Questions with explanations

1. Which House directly determines whether the Union Council of Ministers retains confidence? Lok Sabha.
2. Are Directive Principles directly enforceable in court like Fundamental Rights? No, though they guide governance and interpretation.
3. What does federalism distribute? Public powers among levels of government under the Constitution.
4. Which institutions conduct elections and audit public accounts? Election Commission and CAG respectively.
5. Why are local bodies important? They bring decision-making and services closer to local communities, within law and available resources.
6. Does a government scheme automatically prove a constitutional right has been delivered? No. Measure legal entitlement, actual access, quality, and redress.
