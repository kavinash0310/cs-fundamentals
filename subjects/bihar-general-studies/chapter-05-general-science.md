# Chapter 5 — General Science in Daily Life

## 1. Physics: explain the mechanism

Speed is distance divided by time; velocity includes direction. Acceleration is change in velocity per time. Newton's laws connect motion and force; F=ma gives net force for mass m and acceleration a. Energy is the capacity to cause change. It can change form but is conserved in an isolated system.

Heat flows from a hotter body to a cooler one by conduction, convection, or radiation. Insulators reduce heat transfer; they do not “contain cold.” Electricity requires a closed circuit. Voltage is potential difference, current is charge flow, and resistance opposes current; Ohm's law V=IR applies to an ohmic conductor under suitable conditions.

Light reflects from surfaces and refracts when its speed changes between media. A convex lens can converge rays; a concave lens diverges them. Sound requires a medium and cannot propagate through a vacuum.

## 2. Chemistry: matter and reactions

Elements contain one type of atom; compounds contain elements chemically combined in fixed proportions; mixtures combine substances physically and can often be separated by physical methods. A solution is a homogeneous mixture. Acids and bases change indicators and react in predictable ways; neutralization forms salt and water in common cases.

A chemical equation conserves atoms: coefficients balance the number of each element on both sides. Rusting involves iron reacting with oxygen and water. Combustion needs fuel, oxygen, and ignition energy. Everyday examples help, but distinguish an observation from a molecular explanation.

## 3. Biology and health

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms. The nucleus contains most genetic material in eukaryotic cells; mitochondria release usable energy through cellular respiration; chloroplasts in plants perform photosynthesis. Photosynthesis uses light energy to make sugars from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen.

The circulatory system transports materials; respiratory organs exchange gases; digestion breaks food into absorbable molecules; kidneys filter blood and regulate water and salts; the nervous and endocrine systems coordinate functions. Vaccination prepares immune responses; antibiotics act against bacteria, not viruses. Hygiene, clean water, nutrition, and timely medical advice reduce risk but do not replace clinical care.

## 4. Scientific reasoning

A fair comparison changes one factor while keeping relevant conditions alike. Observation records what happened; inference explains it; a hypothesis makes a testable prediction. A correlation between two trends does not prove one caused the other. Repeat measurements and disclose uncertainty.

~~~text
question -> hypothesis -> controlled test -> observations
      -> compare evidence -> revise explanation
~~~

## 5. Questions with explanations

1. Why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden spoon in the same room? Metal conducts heat from the hand more rapidly.
2. Why balance a chemical equation? Matter's atoms are conserved in the reaction.
3. Do antibiotics treat influenza viruses? No; antibiotics target bacteria, and unnecessary use contributes to resistance.
4. What is the role of chlorophyll? It absorbs light energy used in photosynthesis.
5. Is correlation proof of causation? No; confounders or reverse causation may explain an association.
6. What makes an experiment fair? Relevant variables are controlled so the factor under investigation can be evaluated.
