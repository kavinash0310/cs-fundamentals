# Chapter 1 — Verbal Aptitude

## 1. Grammar for meaning
Review subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, articles, prepositions, pronouns, conjunctions, modifiers, and parallel structure. Identify the true subject even when phrases intervene.

Example: “The list of topics is long.” Subject is list, not topics. In “Each of the answers is checked,” each is singular in standard usage.

## 2. Vocabulary in context
Infer meaning from sentence clues, contrast, examples, and tone. Learn word families and common idioms in context; avoid choosing a familiar meaning that does not fit the sentence.

## 3. Reading comprehension
Read the question, identify passage claim and evidence, separate fact from inference, and reject options that add unsupported assumptions. For “main idea,” choose the broad claim supported by the whole passage. For “inference,” choose what must or is strongly supported, not merely plausible.

## 4. Narrative sequencing
Arrange sentences by pronoun reference, chronology, cause-effect, and transition words. Find opening context and closing consequence; test whether each sentence follows coherently.

## 5. Questions and practice
**Q1.** Choose correct: “Neither of the plans ___ ready.” **A:** is.
**Q2.** What should a comprehension answer rely on? **A:** Evidence in the passage.
**Q3.** What clue can resolve pronoun order? **A:** An explicit antecedent earlier in the sequence.
Practice: edit a paragraph for agreement; infer a word from context; summarize a passage in one sentence; order four events.
