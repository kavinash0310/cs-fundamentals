# Chapter 4 — Timed Practice and Error Review

## 1. A practical solving loop
1. Read exactly what is asked.
2. Write a short representation: equation, diagram, table, or sentence logic.
3. Estimate scale before calculation.
4. Solve with units and intermediate steps.
5. Check edge conditions and whether the answer is plausible.
6. If stuck, skip and return; avoid spending disproportionate time on one item.

## 2. Error categories
Classify misses: concept gap, misread condition, arithmetic slip, unit error, unsupported inference, or time pressure. Each category needs a different fix. Re-solve missed items after a delay rather than memorizing the answer.

## 3. Mixed mini-set
1. 15% of 240 = **36**.
2. If all roses are flowers and some flowers fade quickly, must some roses fade quickly? **No; not enough information.**
3. A value rises 50% then falls 50%. Net change? **25% decrease** (1.5×0.5=0.75).
4. Number of ways to choose 2 from 6? **15.**
5. A fair coin is tossed 3 times; exactly 2 heads? **C(3,2)/8=3/8.**
6. A bar chart axis begins at 90 and shows 95 vs 100. Why inspect scale? **Truncated axis can visually exaggerate difference.**
7. Two workers complete a job in 6 hours together; their individual rates sum to 1/6 job/hour. More information is needed for each separate rate.
8. A 90° counterclockwise rotation followed by 90° clockwise rotation returns the original shape.
9. In “The group of students are/is ready,” in formal singular collective usage choose **is**; usage may depend on dialect and meaning.
10. For comprehension, choose the claim supported by the passage rather than outside knowledge.

## Notebook work
After each timed set, record score, time, and error type. Revisit the weakest area with untimed practice before taking another timed set.


## Logic and spatial worked checks

Deduction: “All P are Q; some Q are R” does not imply some P are R; the Q∩R objects may be outside P. Draw overlapping circles to test whether conclusion is forced.

Induction in patterns: sequence 2,5,10,17,26 has differences 3,5,7,9; a plausible next term is 37 (add 11), but always verify whether another rule fits.

Paper folding: if a square is folded left-to-right, a hole at a point off the crease mirrors to the corresponding point in the other half after unfolding. For multiple folds, reverse the folds in reverse order; each crease creates mirrored copies.

**Practice:** Test syllogisms with a Venn diagram; rotate a shape 180°; reflect across vertical axis; fold twice and predict number/locations of holes. State why a guessed number pattern is not a proof of a unique next term.

## Extended lesson: mixed timed set and review

Try these without looking at the answers; target about one minute each.

1. 15% of 240 = ?
2. A 120 m train passes a pole in 6 s. Speed in km/h?
3. If 3 workers finish a job in 8 days, at equal rates how long for 4 workers?
4. Complete: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, __.
5. All A are B; no B are C. Can any A be C?
6. A fair coin is tossed twice. Probability of exactly one head?
7. Mean of 4, 6, 8, 10?
8. A value rises from 80 to 100. Percentage increase?
9. Which is larger: 3/7 or 4/9?
10. A cube is painted and cut 3×3×3. How many pieces have exactly one painted face?

### Answers and reasoning

1. 0.15×240=36.
2. 120/6=20 m/s; multiply by 3.6 to get 72 km/h.
3. Total work=24 worker-days; 24/4=6 days.
4. Differences are 4,6,8,10; next difference 12, so 42.
5. No; A is contained in B and B is disjoint from C.
6. Outcomes HH, HT, TH, TT; two of four, so 1/2.
7. (4+6+8+10)/4=7.
8. Increase=20; base=80; 20/80=25%.
9. Cross-multiply: 3×9=27, 4×7=28, so 4/9 is larger.
10. 6(n-2)^2=6 for n=3.

### Build an error log

For every miss, record the skill, the exact mistaken assumption, and a corrected method. Example: “Used final value as percent denominator” becomes “percentage change divides by original value.” Re-solve missed items after a day without viewing the solution. Track accuracy by topic, not just total score; a strong total can hide a repeat weakness.

During the exam, take quick marks from clear questions first, then return to lengthy items. Do not spend several minutes defending one sunk-cost question. If negative marking applies, use the stated scoring rule rather than a blanket guessing rule.
