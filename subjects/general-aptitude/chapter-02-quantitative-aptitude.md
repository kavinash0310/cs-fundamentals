# Chapter 2 — Quantitative Aptitude and Data Interpretation

## 1. Ratios, percentages, and rates
Ratio a:b compares quantities; scale both by the same nonzero factor. Percentage change = (new-old)/old×100%. Successive changes multiply: +10% then -10% gives 1.1×0.9=0.99, a 1% decrease.

Speed=distance/time; work rate adds for independent workers. Keep units consistent.

## 2. Powers, logarithms, and estimation
Rules: aᵐaⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ; log(ab)=log a+log b; log(aᵏ)=k log a. Estimate by rounding only after preserving the needed precision and order of magnitude. Check whether a result is plausible.

## 3. Counting and series
Use permutations when order matters, combinations when it does not. Arithmetic sequence adds common difference; geometric sequence multiplies common ratio. Sum formulas:
~~~text
1+...+n = n(n+1)/2
geometric: a+ar+...+ar^(n-1)=a(r^n-1)/(r-1), r≠1
~~~

## 4. Geometry, statistics, probability
Area rectangle=lw, triangle=bh/2, circle=πr²; volume prism=base area×height, cylinder=πr²h. Mean=sum/count; median is middle after sorting. Probability of equally likely event= favorable/total. Use units and diagrams.

## 5. Data interpretation
Read graph title, axes, units, legend, and scale before calculating. For pie chart, sector percentage=angle/360×100. Distinguish absolute change from percentage change and verify table totals.

Example: sales rise from 80 to 100: increase 20, percentage rise 25%, not 20%.

## 6. Questions and practice
**Q1.** 20% of 150? **A:** 30.
**Q2.** 3 workers finish in 8 days at equal rates. How long for 6 workers? **A:** 4 days, assuming same efficiency.
**Q3.** Median of 2,3,3,10,12? **A:** 3.
**Q4.** Probability of sum 7 on two fair dice? **A:** 6/36=1/6.
Practice: interpret a bar chart; solve percentage and ratio problems; estimate a square root; distinguish combinations from permutations.

## Reading-comprehension worked method

Passage claim: “Urban trees reduce heat, but benefits vary by species and placement.” A supported inference is that planting location matters. “All cities should plant only one species” is unsupported and contradicts variation.

For sentence correction, first find subject and main verb, then check agreement and tense. Ignore intervening phrases: “The quality of the examples is/are important”—head noun quality is singular, so is.

For sequencing, link pronouns to antecedents and transitions:
1. introduce the object/person,
2. describe an action,
3. use “this result” to refer to that action,
4. conclude with consequence.
Chronology alone may not determine order.

**Practice:** Summarize a 4-sentence paragraph; identify one inference and one fact; edit “Each of the students have submitted their work” according to formal test convention (has).
